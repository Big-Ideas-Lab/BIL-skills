# Hypothesis classes

These classes apply to the **shortlist** in the decision-grade lane — not to every idea generated during brainstorming. Forcing five classes onto a divergent candidate list produces filler, and filler hides the real alternatives.

## The five classes

| Class | Purpose | Required content |
|---|---|---|
| **focal** | The hypothesis under primary consideration | Mechanism, expected pattern, decisive comparison |
| **rival** | A different mechanism that produces the same observation | The mechanism, and the observation that separates it from the focal |
| **null** | No effect, or the effect is fully redundant with an existing metric | The baseline it must beat, and the equivalence or non-inferiority framing |
| **artifact / confounding** | The pattern is produced by measurement, sampling, processing, or a third variable | The specific artifact or confounder and how it would be detected |
| **high-upside** | Expensive, uncertain, but would materially change what the group can claim if true | Why the cost is justified, and the earliest point at which it could be abandoned |

## Why the rival matters most

A focal hypothesis plus a rival is the minimum honest pair. Without a rival, a positive result cannot be distinguished from the alternative explanation, and the group learns less than the result appears to show.

## Selecting the shortlist

Shortlist a generated candidate when at least one is true:

- it is Pareto-nondominated on the four axes;
- it is the cheapest available falsification of an important claim;
- it protects other hypotheses from a shared failure mode (typical for artifact/confounding candidates);
- it gates the feasibility of everything else downstream (typical for validation hypotheses).

Do not shortlist a candidate only because it is the requester's preference. If the requester insists, record it as a stated preference, not as an axis rating.

## Class coverage check

For every shortlisted focal hypothesis, confirm before finishing:

- a **rival** exists and is separately testable;
- a **null** explains why the pattern could mean nothing new;
- an **artifact/confounding** explanation names a specific mechanism, not "bias" in general.

High-upside belongs to the portfolio, not to every focal hypothesis. A portfolio with no high-upside candidate is often a sign of an over-cautious scoping; a portfolio where every candidate is high-upside is usually not a portfolio at all.

## Anti-patterns

- **Token rival.** A rival that cannot actually produce the observation.
- **Null as straw man.** A null compared against a weak baseline instead of the strongest existing metric.
- **Artifact vague.** "There might be confounding" with no named confounder or detection path.
- **High-upside as wish.** No abandonment criterion and no earliest decision point.
- **Class inflation.** Five classes written for a hypothesis that only needed a focal and a rival.
