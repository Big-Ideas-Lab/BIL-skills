01 Topic and scope

Research direction (fixed input): derive interpretable glucose-excursion phenotypes from continuous glucose monitoring and test whether they carry incremental cardiometabolic information beyond established summary metrics.

In scope: excursion detection and characterization from CGM-only input; within-person and between-person comparisons where the design supports them; explicit measurement validation before outcome association.

Out of scope: causal claims about diet or clinical events without reference labels; device-level algorithmic claims; and any advocation of a single direction.

  • Inferential boundary: an algorithm-defined excursion is not a meal, exposure, diagnosis, or response until reference evidence supports the attribution.
  • Access boundary: controlled-data analyses and external outreach are separate authorizations from this report.

02 Evidence brief

Existing work establishes that CGM-derived variability summaries associate with cardiometabolic outcomes, while excursion-level phenotyping remains less standardized and largely unvalidated against reference labels.

  • Established: sensor-derived variability summaries and time-in-range metrics have reproducible associations with glycaemic control.
  • Contested: whether excursion shape and timing add information beyond those summaries, and how much of that signal is measurement artifact.
  • Gap: reference-labelled datasets that let an excursion detector be validated before it is used as a phenotype.

Known limitation

  • Full text for two sources remains awaiting_full_text; no claim in this report depends on them.
  • One included conference abstract supports discovery only and is not used to verify any claim.

Excursion timing carries incremental within-person information

Excursion timing features predict next-window glycaemic state better than variability summaries alone.

Prediction, falsifier, and alternatives
Prediction
Held-out within-person improvement over a variability-summary baseline.
Falsifier
No held-out improvement beyond the baseline under a person-level split.
Minimum evidence
One existing CGM cohort with person-level splits and a frozen feature set.
